# Pipeline: from story to rendered deck

The talk was a ten-minute camera ride through a space station built in Blender. Each click on the clicker plays the next stretch of the ride. This page walks through the steps in the order they happen. Tool names refer to [../tools/](../tools/README.md) and [../scripts/](../scripts/README.md).

## 1. Story

Start with the story, not the scene: what the audience should feel and understand at each moment, and what the speaker says there. Each click is one beat of the story. A stop exists only where there is something to see or say; corridors, doors and stairs are passed through without stopping (see [camera-rules.md](camera-rules.md)).

## 2. Storyboard

The story becomes a storyboard of still images, one panel per stop. Panels are numbered "scene.shot" (for example `05.2`); a panel inserted later gets a letter (`05.2a`) so the other numbers do not shift during discussion. Each panel carries the shot size (wide, medium, close-up), the camera angle, how the camera arrives (static, dolly, track, pan, tilt, cut), what should be seen, the spoken text, the duration, and what is still missing in the scene.

The images are not drawn: they are rendered from the grey-box scene with the camera of the final film, small and fast (960 x 540, few samples). That makes the storyboard a technical previsualisation: it shows exactly what the lens will see, and it shows which parts of the set never come into view and need not be built. The storyboard lives in one data file ([../scripts/data/storyboard_station.yaml](../scripts/data/storyboard_station.yaml)) that the scene script reads.

## 3. Scene script

The whole scene is generated by Python scripts inside Blender ([../scripts/scene/station_scene.py](../scripts/scene/station_scene.py)); nothing is modelled by hand. Exhibits, panels and camera stops come from the storyboard and layout data. Rebuilding the scene from scripts on every run keeps every change traceable and lets an agent change what it can write. The tools are started through a small runner ([../tools/blender_run.py](../tools/blender_run.py)) that converts the YAML settings into JSON for Blender's Python.

## 4. Ride check

Before anything is rendered at full quality, the ride is computed and checked in numbers:

- the camera path and its speeds, turns, horizon and holds against the camera rules ([../scripts/scene/check_motion.py](../scripts/scene/check_motion.py));
- per panel: nothing opaque closer than half a metre in the field of view, a free line of sight to the target, the target fully in frame with a margin, no exhibit cutting through a wall unless intended, the image not nearly one colour;
- a contact sheet of all stops to look at, plus a low frame rate preview video for movement (movement can only be judged as video, never as a sequence of stills).

Content checks can run at about one frame per second; movement previews at 12 frames per second are enough to judge the ride.

## 5. Render in click segments

The final render is cut into segments, one per click: from leaving one stop to leaving the next. Each segment gets its own folder, is named after its panels (not after frame numbers, which shift with every change before it) and stores a `segment.json` with its frames and a fingerprint of its camera path. Benefits:

- an aborted run loses nothing: existing images are skipped, the next run fills the gaps;
- a change to one room re-renders only the segments of that room;
- the deck can take finished segments and use previews for the rest, so it improves piece by piece.

Holds where nothing moves are not rendered out frame by frame: a still hold is rendered as three frames, and if they are equal the clip ends on the arrival image and PowerPoint holds it. Only holds with movement in the picture (a video on a screen, a page turning, a door, a rotating object) are rendered for their full length. The final hold of the talk is a short seamless loop instead of two minutes of frames.

Contiguous runs of movement are rendered as animations; segment boundaries lie only where the camera rests, so the cut between two clips is invisible.

**Fingerprints and shifted timelines.** The fingerprint of a segment is computed from its camera path relative to its start. When a change early in the ride shifts the timeline, a later segment can keep the same path and therefore the same fingerprint, while its frame numbers change. Lesson learned the night before the talk: a segment with an unchanged fingerprint was counted as finished although its files still carried the old frame numbers, and the deck mixed old and new folders. Two consequences:

- after a timeline shift, renumber the existing images of unchanged segments instead of re-rendering them, and never trust frame numbers alone;
- better still, number frames relative to the start of each segment, and keep the camera path of every run (each run used to overwrite the previous path file, which would have made a whole set of finished images unusable).

**Frame-exact re-render.** After a correction, [../tools/rerender_plan.py](../tools/rerender_plan.py) compares the new ride with the finished images. An image is re-rendered only if its camera pose changed or a changed object is visible in it (plus a small margin of frames before and after); images whose pose is unchanged but whose number shifted are renamed (as hard links, no extra space). Affected segments move to an `_old/` folder first, nothing is deleted. Limits: light or reflections of a changed object on other surfaces are not detected; mark such areas with a box by hand.

## 6. Render queue

Long renders must not depend on an interactive session. [../tools/night_queue.py](../tools/night_queue.py) runs the jobs of a jobs file in a plain console window: it moves half-written images aside before starting, restarts crashed runs a few times, reports progress and an estimate every few minutes, writes small preview images for every finished segment, and accepts `clean` (finish running jobs, then stop), `shutdown` and `status`.

Measure before you render: disk space per frame (PNG at 1080p was about 2.6 MB) and render time per area (interiors took seconds per frame, the large hub twenty), then decide storage, order and schedule. Render the longest segments first, so an interruption hits only short ones. Do not split the work into fixed halves at the start: one half may take hours longer and the other worker then sits idle; let each free worker take the next open segment. Two renders sharing one GPU, or a render next to other GPU work, slow each other down; a simple GPU queue that hands out slots avoids overloading the machine.

## 7. Deck from pieces

[../tools/build_pptx.py](../tools/build_pptx.py) builds the PowerPoint deck: one slide per click, each with an embedded MP4 clip encoded by ffmpeg from the segment's images. The slide gets the clip's first image as background and poster frame, the video starts by itself and stays on its last image. The last image of one clip is the first image of the next, so the slide change cannot be seen. A closing overlay (web address and speaker name) is laid over the final loop as PowerPoint shapes, so it stays editable.

## 8. Cut check

[../tools/check_deck_cuts.py](../tools/check_deck_cuts.py) decodes every clip and compares, frame by frame: the first image with the image of its stop, the last image with the next stop, the clip length with the expected frame count, and the last image of each clip with the first of the next. One frame off gives a clearly higher difference with a moving camera than encoding noise does. The report is written next to the deck.

## 9. Fallbacks

Projectors, laptops and embedded video fail on stage. [../tools/build_fallbacks.py](../tools/build_fallbacks.py) writes two fallbacks next to the deck: one video with all clips in order (no re-encoding), and a PowerPoint with one still image per click, to click through without any video.

## 10. Credits overlay

Third-party works seen in the ride are credited on screen as chat messages, from the first frame they are visible. The overlay is burnt into the clips after rendering, so it can be fixed without rendering the scene again. Details: [credits-overlay.md](credits-overlay.md); tools [../tools/credit_overlay.py](../tools/credit_overlay.py) and [../tools/credit_ride.py](../tools/credit_ride.py).
